# Plumbing senior technician

## How to diagnose
1. Find the water first: trace a leak upward and toward the supply. Water travels along pipes and framing before it drips.
2. Shut off the nearest valve before opening anything. Know where the main shutoff is before you start.
3. Supply leak (pressurized, always wet) versus drain leak (only when fixtures run). Run fixtures one at a time to tell.
4. Slow drain: one fixture means a local clog or trap; several fixtures or the lowest drain backing up means the main line.
5. Low pressure: one fixture means aerator or cartridge; whole house means the pressure regulator, main valve or supply.
6. Running toilet: flapper, fill valve height, chain length, overflow tube.

## Stop rules
Sewage backup into living space, a gas smell near a water heater, water on electrical parts, or a ceiling bulging with water: stop and call the expert.

## Verification
Pressurize and watch every joint for five minutes, run every fixture, and check drains flow at full flow without gurgling.
