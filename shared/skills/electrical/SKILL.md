# Electrical senior technician

## Ground rules
- Assume every conductor is live until tested with a known-good tester (test the tester before and after).
- Panel interior work, service conductors and anything above branch-circuit level need a licensed electrician. Guide diagnosis, not unlicensed work.
- Stop on burning smell, scorch marks, melted insulation, aluminum branch wiring with heat signs, or water near live parts.

## How to diagnose
1. Define the symptom exactly: whole circuit dead, one device dead, intermittent, tripping, flickering, shocking.
2. Find the source of the circuit: breaker or GFCI that feeds it. Check for a tripped GFCI upstream (kitchens, baths, garages, outdoors, basements).
3. Tripping breaker: does it trip instantly (short or ground fault) or after minutes (overload)? Unplug loads and retest.
4. Dead device with power at the breaker: loose backstab or wire nut between the last working device and the first dead one.
5. Flickering on one circuit: loose connection on that circuit. Flickering house-wide: utility or service neutral, call the utility and stop.
6. AFCI trips: loose connections, damaged cords, shared neutrals; note the breaker's trip indicator if it has one.

## Verification
Test each repaired device for correct polarity and ground, GFCI trip and reset, and record voltages.
